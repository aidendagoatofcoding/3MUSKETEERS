3MUSKETTERS 
Complete Base44 Game-Building Prompt 
Instructions: Paste the prompt below into Base44. Build a playable, persistent, multiplayer top-down 2D action game inspired by the progression and feel of florr.io, but centered on historical-fantasy soldiers, swords, muskets, armor, mobs, portals, clans, upgrades, and loot. 
1. Core Game Concept 
Create a game called 3MUSKETTERS. It is a top-down 2D multiplayer action and progression game inspired by florr.io. The player controls a soldier—not a flower or nature creature—in a large world. Players explore, fight themed enemies, collect weapons and armor, level up, craft stronger equipment, enter new worlds through portals, join factions and clans, fight bosses, and compete with other real players. 
Use responsive WASD and arrow-key movement. The camera smoothly follows the player. Support desktop controls first, with a layout that can adapt to mobile later. The game should feel lively and easy to understand, with satisfying combat feedback and clear progression. 
2. Player Characters and Customization 
Players begin by choosing or creating a soldier. Include historical-inspired character styles such as: 
British: Redcoat Infantry, Grenadier, Officer, Cavalry Soldier, Rifleman, Royal Guard, Highlander, Naval Marine, Commander. 
Japanese: Samurai, Ashigaru Spearman, Ashigaru Matchlock Soldier, Samurai Commander, Ronin, Shogun Guard, Imperial Guard, Samurai Archer, Elite Samurai. 
Other styles: French Napoleonic Infantry, Prussian Musketeer, Spanish Soldier, Ottoman Janissary, Pirate, Viking, Medieval Knight, Mongol Warrior, Imperial Guard, and Mercenary. 
Allow cosmetic customization of hats, helmets, uniforms, armor, boots, backpacks, masks, capes, insignias, and clan emblems. Character appearance should update immediately when equipment or cosmetics change. Cosmetics should not secretly provide combat advantages unless explicitly designated as functional armor. 
3. Factions 
Create selectable factions: British Empire, Japanese Shogunate, French Empire, Prussian Kingdom, Ottoman Empire, Spanish Empire, Pirate Confederation, and Independent Ronin. 
Each faction has its own colors, emblem, cosmetic options, faction identity, statistics, achievements, and leaderboard. Players may use any weapon regardless of faction. Factions should be primarily social and competitive identity choices; do not give one faction an unfair combat advantage. 
4. Weapons: Swords 
Swords orbit around the player in a style inspired by florr.io. When an orbiting sword collides with an enemy or another player in a combat-enabled area, it deals damage based on its stats. Support multiple equipped swords, with a configurable equipment limit. 
Traditional swords: Wooden Sword, Stone Sword, Iron Sword, Steel Sword, Bronze Sword, Silver Sword, Golden Sword, Diamond Sword, Obsidian Sword, Crystal Sword, Ancient Sword, Knight Sword, Royal Sword, Greatsword, Claymore, Rapier,

Saber, Cutlass, Scimitar, Broadsword, Shortsword, Longsword, Bastard Sword, Zweihander, Executioner's Sword. 
Japanese swords: Katana, Wakizashi, Tanto, Nodachi, Odachi, Tachi, Uchigatana, Dual Katanas, Black Katana, White Katana, Red Katana, Golden Katana, Dragon Katana, Cursed Katana, Flaming Katana, Frozen Katana, Lightning Katana, Shadow Katana, Celestial Katana, Samurai Greatsword and the ultra rare triple blade katana
Fantasy swords: Flame Sword, Frost Sword, Thunder Sword, Poison Sword, Venom Blade, Shadow Blade, Holy Sword, Demon Sword, Angelic Sword, Dragon Slayer, Soul Reaper, Void Blade, Star Sword, Moonblade, Sunblade, Galaxy Sword, Cosmic Blade, Dimension Cutter, Time Sword, Reality Blade, Infinite Blade, Bloodstone Blade, Meteor Sword, Chainsword, Sawblade Sword, Energy Sword, Plasma Blade, Laser Sword, Bone Sword, Thorn Sword, Crystal Greatsword, Ancient Relic Sword. 
Every sword needs its own name, icon or sprite, rarity, damage, attack behavior, visual effects, and optional special ability. Balance weapons so that higher rarity usually means stronger or more unusual effects, without making early progression impossible. 
5. Weapons: Muskets and Firearms 
Muskets are a separate weapon category. They automatically target nearby enemies within their configured range and fire visible projectiles. Support multiple equipped muskets, subject to a configurable equipment limit. Use stylized game effects; do not simulate real-world firearm operation. 
Historical firearms: Wooden Musket, Matchlock Musket, Flintlock Musket, Brown Bess, Charleville Musket, Springfield Musket, Kentucky Long Rifle, Baker Rifle, Blunderbuss, Double-Barrel Musket, Heavy Musket, Hunting Musket, Royal Musket, Cavalry Musket, Pirate Musket, Naval Musket, Officer's Pistol, Flintlock Pistol, Repeating Musket. 
Japanese firearms: Japanese Matchlock, Tanegashima, Samurai Matchlock, Ashigaru Musket, Ornate Matchlock, Golden Matchlock, Imperial Matchlock, Dragon Matchlock, Shogun Musket, Cursed Matchlock. 
Special and fantasy firearms: Golden Musket, Silver Musket, Diamond Musket, Crystal Musket, Ancient Musket, Royal Musket, Knight's Musket, Dragon Musket, Pirate Musket, Imperial Musket, Fire Musket, Ice Musket, Lightning Musket, Poison Musket, Shadow Musket, Holy Musket, Crystal Musket, Magma Musket, Frost Musket, Thunder Musket, Void Musket, Celestial Musket, Plasma Musket, Laser Musket, Energy Musket, Star Musket, Galaxy Musket, Cosmic Musket, Dimension Musket, Soul Musket, Phantom Musket, Time Musket, Reality Musket, Infinite Musket, flamethrower 
Each musket has damage, range, projectile speed, cooldown, projectile count, rarity, appearance, and possibly a special effect. Some may fire multiple projectiles, pierce targets, bounce, or create a stylized area effect. Make projectiles visible and readable. Also make the weapons balanced mostly with trade offs but some are still better than others
6. Rarity System 
Use these item and mob rarities: Common, Unusual, Rare, Epic, Legendary, Mythic, Ultra, Super, Unique, Divine, and Celestial. Give each rarity a distinct color, visual treatment, and appropriate stat range. Higher rarities should be harder to obtain and more visually impressive. 
Important: Mob rarity and item rarity are independent. A Legendary mob can drop a Common item, and a Common mob can very rarely drop a Celestial item. Do not guarantee that an item matches the rarity of the mob that dropped it. 
7. Mobs and Enemies — Soldier and Weapon Theme Only 
Remove all old nature-themed and generic object enemies. Do not include rocks, bushes, cacti, flowers, ladybugs, bees, ants, beetles, spiders, or similar nature-themed mobs. Every ordinary enemy should fit the historical soldier, warrior, pirate, armor, or fantasy-weapon theme. 
British-themed mobs: Rogue Redcoat, Deserting Soldier, British Grenadier, Royal Guard, Rogue Officer, British Cavalryman, Royal Musketeer, Armored Redcoat, British Commander, Royal General.

Japanese-themed mobs: Rogue Samurai, Ronin, Ashigaru, Samurai Archer, Matchlock Soldier, Samurai Commander, Armored Samurai, Oni Warrior, Demon Samurai, Shogun Guardian. 
Medieval-themed mobs: Swordsman, Knight, Black Knight, Templar, Crusader, Bandit, Mercenary, Executioner, Armored Knight, Royal Knight, Paladin, Dark Knight. 
Pirate-themed mobs: Pirate, Pirate Captain, Cutlass Fighter, Pirate Musketeer, Buccaneer, Pirate Veteran, Pirate Admiral, Ghost Pirate, Cursed Captain. 
European and other military mobs: French Infantryman, French Grenadier, Prussian Musketeer, Spanish Swordsman, Ottoman Janissary, Imperial Guardsman, Highland Warrior, Royal Cavalryman, Elite Musketeer. 
Fantasy weapon and armor mobs: Living Sword, Flying Katana, Walking Musket, Animated Armor, Haunted Helmet, Sword Golem, Musket Golem, Blade Spirit, Cannon Creature, Shield Guardian, Iron Knight, Golden Knight, Crystal Samurai, Shadow Musketeer, Phantom Swordsman, Dragon Knight. 
Give each mob a clear appearance, name, rarity, health, damage, movement speed, attack style, XP reward, world spawn rules, and individual loot table. Mobs should have readable animations and combat behavior. Avoid making enemies visually indistinguishable. 
8. Bosses and Raids 
Add large boss enemies with visible health bars, distinctive appearances, multiple readable attack patterns, and group-friendly difficulty. Bosses include Redcoat Commander, Shogun, Pirate Admiral, Black Knight, Samurai General, French Emperor, Ottoman Warlord, Phantom Musketeer, Dragon Samurai, and Celestial General. 
Bosses should award XP and have a chance to drop exclusive weapons, armor, crafting materials, and cosmetics. Make boss encounters feel special, but do not make any single boss reward mandatory for basic progression. Support group encounters and clear boss spawn or event announcements. 
9. Equal Overall Loot Chance and Individual Mob Loot Tables 
Most important loot rule: Every defeated mob must use the same overall probability of dropping at least one item, regardless of the mob's type or rarity. Use a shared configurable base chance of 50% for every mob. This means every mob has the same 50% chance to drop something and the same 50% chance to drop nothing. Store this as one global balance setting so it can be changed later. Do not give rare mobs a higher overall chance of dropping anything. 
If the shared loot roll succeeds, select the item from that specific mob's own loot table. The contents of the loot table can differ by mob. A mob's signature or primary weapon should have the highest individual chance. Other swords and muskets should be possible but less likely. Armor should sometimes drop, but it must not be guaranteed. Materials or cosmetics can also appear where appropriate. 
Example: Rogue Redcoat loot table (when the global loot roll succeeds): 40% Redcoat Saber or Brown Bess (signature items); 20% other swords; 15% other muskets; 15% Redcoat armor pieces or uniform cosmetics; 10% materials or other eligible rewards. 
Example: Rogue Samurai loot table (when the global loot roll succeeds): 40% Katana or Samurai Matchlock (signature items); 20% other swords; 15% other muskets; 15% Samurai armor pieces; 10% materials or other eligible rewards. 
Example: Knight loot table (when the global loot roll succeeds): 45% Longsword or Knight's Sword (signature items); 15% other swords; 10% muskets; 20% knight armor pieces; 10% materials or other eligible rewards. 
Example: Pirate loot table (when the global loot roll succeeds): 40% Cutlass or Pirate Musket (signature items); 20% other swords; 15% other muskets; 15% pirate armor or clothing pieces; 10% materials or other eligible rewards. 
These example percentages are conditional on a drop occurring and should be configurable per mob. The global 50% chance to drop something remains identical for all mobs. Within a category, select an eligible item from that mob's list. Do not make armor guaranteed or make every mob drop the same items.

After selecting the item, roll its rarity independently using this shared default distribution: Common 60%, Unusual 20%, Rare 10%, Epic 5%, Legendary 2.5%, Mythic 1.2%, Ultra 0.7%, Super 0.3%, Unique 0.15%, Divine 0.1%, Celestial 0.05%. These weights total 100%. Allow item-specific overrides only when needed for balance, and keep rarity independent from mob rarity. 
Show dropped items clearly in the world with rarity-colored effects. Let players collect drops through proximity or a clear interaction. Prevent duplicate or double-awarded loot through server-side validation. Make loot results fair in multiplayer and ensure one player's reward is not accidentally granted to every nearby player. 
10. Armor and Equipment 
Add equippable armor. Armor is a real equipment category, not just a cosmetic drop. Include helmet or hat, chest armor or uniform, leg armor, boots, gloves or gauntlets, shield, cape, backpack, and accessory slots as appropriate. 
Armor can affect maximum health, damage reduction, movement speed, and selected resistances. Balance trade-offs carefully; heavy armor may provide more protection but reduce movement speed. Armor should visibly change the soldier's appearance. 
Add set bonuses for wearing multiple pieces from the same set. Example sets: Redcoat Uniform, Samurai Armor, Knight Plate, Pirate Outfit, French Imperial Uniform, Prussian Armor, Ottoman Janissary Armor, Highland Gear, Phantom Armor, Dragon Armor, and Celestial Armor. 
Armor can be obtained from mob loot tables, bosses, crafting, and special world rewards. Armor should be possible but not guaranteed from mobs, following the individual loot-table rules above. Give armor its own rarity, stats, icon, description, and collection entry. 
11. Worlds and Portals 
Create multiple explorable worlds connected by animated portals. Each world should have a unique visual theme, enemy roster, boss, crafting materials, loot pool, difficulty, and recommended level. 
Worlds: British Countryside; Japanese Province; European Battlefield; Medieval Kingdom; Pirate Islands; Desert Empire; Frozen Fortress; Volcanic Stronghold; Crystal Caverns; Shadow Realm; Celestial Empire. 
Portals should have clear visual effects, labels, and level requirements. Make world transitions reliable and preserve the player's saved progress and equipment. Do not populate these worlds with the removed nature-themed mob roster. 
12. Upgrades and Leveling 
Defeating mobs and completing events grants XP. Leveling up grants upgrade points. Include upgrades for movement speed, maximum health, health regeneration, inventory capacity, damage, attack speed, critical chance, XP gain, and loot luck which is all stored in a map where you need to un
Upgrade costs should increase as levels rise. Add sensible caps and diminishing returns where necessary. Save all upgrade choices to the player's account. Armor and weapons can also affect stats, but show the source of each stat bonus clearly. 
13. Inventory and Collection 
Create an inventory for swords, muskets, armor, materials, and cosmetics. Let players equip and unequip multiple weapons within configurable slot limits. Include sorting, filtering, item comparison, storage, inventory expansion, and item detail panels. 
Each item detail panel should show its name, rarity, category, stats, special ability, and source. Add a collection encyclopedia with undiscovered silhouettes, discovered item details, the source mob or world, and collection completion progress. 
14. Crafting and Upgrading Items 
Allow players to combine items and materials to attempt to create a higher-rarity item. A basic recipe may require three items of the same rarity plus materials or coins. Support crafting for swords, muskets, and armor.

Crafting must not always succeed. Use these default success rates when attempting the next rarity: Common to Unusual 90%; Unusual to Rare 80%; Rare to Epic 65%; Epic to Legendary 45%; Legendary to Mythic 30%; Mythic to Ultra 20%; Ultra to Super 12%; Super to Unique 8%; Unique to Divine 4%; Divine to Celestial 1%. 
On failure, apply a clearly displayed and configurable consequence, such as consuming the materials or destroying some of the contributed items. Special materials may improve success chance. Perform all random rolls on the server, show the odds before the player confirms, and record crafting history. Add a satisfying crafting animation and clear success/failure feedback. 
15. Clans 
Players can create, join, leave, and invite others to clans. Support clan names, short tags, emblems, colors, descriptions, member lists, and role ranks such as Leader, Officer, and Member. Leaders and officers should have configurable permissions. 
Include clan chat, clan statistics, achievements, a clan leaderboard, member management, and clan-versus-clan events. Add territory competitions only if they can be implemented fairly and reliably. Support a configurable member limit and optional clan upgrades. 
16. Multiplayer and PvP 
Real multiplayer is required. Do not simulate other players with bots or fake names. Multiple real users must be able to join the same game world, see one another move, fight mobs, and interact in real time. 
Use server-authoritative logic for player movement validation, combat, damage, loot rolls, inventory changes, crafting, and persistent progression. Do not trust the client to award items or decide damage. 
Support player health, combat feedback, kill notifications, leaderboards, and optional PvP zones. Include spawn protection, safe zones, and clear indicators when PvP is enabled. If a player is defeated in PvP, use a balanced and clearly explained partial-loss or drop rule; do not erase all progress. Make sure PvP can be disabled in designated safe areas. 
Swords deal collision or orbit-based damage. Muskets fire visible projectiles. Keep combat responsive and readable, with server-side checks to prevent cheating, duplicate rewards, and impossible attack rates. 
17. User Interface and Menus 
Main menu buttons: Play, Inventory, Upgrades, Crafting, Clans, Factions, Leaderboard, Collection, and Settings. Every button must work and open the correct screen. 
In gameplay, display player health, XP progress, level, equipped weapons, current world, inventory capacity, player name, clan and faction, and a minimap. Include readable enemy health bars, boss health bars, item pickup notifications, damage numbers, and portal labels. 
Make the interface clean and easy to navigate. Use consistent rarity colors and clear tooltips. Include settings for sound, music, visual effects, and control preferences. 
18. Art Direction, Animation, and Sound 
Use a polished top-down 2D visual style. Player characters should clearly look like soldiers or warriors, not flowers. Use recognizable historical-inspired uniforms, helmets, hats, armor, and faction colors. 
Animate walking, attacks, musket firing, projectiles, sword orbiting, enemy movement, damage, defeat, boss attacks, portals, and item drops. Add rarity glows, faction flags, clan emblems, and clear hit effects. 
Use original or properly licensed sound effects for sword hits, musket shots, pickups, crafting, level-ups, portals, and boss events. Provide volume controls and avoid excessively loud defaults.

19. Data, Accounts, and Persistence 
Create persistent data models for accounts, player stats, inventory, equipped items, armor, upgrades, crafting history, clans and members, factions, mobs, weapons, armor definitions, loot tables, worlds, leaderboards, and achievements. 
Save progress reliably. Validate all important actions on the server, especially loot, crafting, currency, equipment, XP, and combat. Handle disconnects and reconnects without losing saved progress or duplicating rewards. Do not expose private account data. 
20. Performance, Balance, and Reliability 
Optimize for many players, mobs, projectiles, and orbiting weapons on screen. Use efficient collision checks, sensible spawn limits, cleanup of expired projectiles and effects, and server-side rate limits. Keep the game playable on ordinary desktop hardware. 
Balance enemy health, damage, speed, XP, weapon stats, armor bonuses, drop tables, crafting odds, and world difficulty. Make early progression achievable while keeping top-tier items rare. Add clear error handling and loading states. Do not leave menu buttons, inventory actions, portals, or crafting controls as decorative nonfunctional elements. 
21. Recommended Build Order 
Build in this order, keeping each stage playable before adding the next: (1) player movement and camera; (2) world map and soldier visuals; (3) sword orbit and basic combat; (4) muskets and projectiles; (5) soldier-themed mobs; (6) mob rarity and enemy behavior; (7) shared global loot chance and individual mob loot tables; (8) item rarity; (9) armor and equipment; (10) inventory and collection; (11) leveling and upgrades; (12) crafting; (13) portals and worlds; (14) bosses and raids; (15) factions; (16) clans; (17) real multiplayer and persistence; (18) PvP, leaderboards, achievements, polish, and performance improvements. 
Prioritize a working playable core over a large collection of unfinished screens. If a feature cannot be completed in the first pass, create a clearly labeled functional placeholder and do not pretend that it is implemented.
